About the Project

What is Saving of Swedovia?

Saving of Swedovia (SOS) is a research game currently in development. The game places participants in the roles of key actors in crisis management — such as firefighters, police officers, and municipal officials — who must make difficult decisions during an ongoing wildfire in the fictional city of Swedovia.

Every decision in the game carries ethical consequences and impacts society at large. When decision-makers and rescue personnel must balance democratic values, professional ethics, and ethical principles under time pressure and uncertainty, there is a risk of both moral stress and inadequate crisis management. Despite this, there is currently limited knowledge about how ethical dilemmas are actually handled in practice, what consequences different priorities have for the overall crisis response, and how citizens perceive what constitutes legitimate decisions when fundamental values collide.

The project's overarching purpose is to contribute new knowledge about how ethical dilemmas are handled in climate crises and how decision-making can strengthen both individual and societal resilience.

Research Phases

Phase 1

Literature Review & Theoretical Framework

Systematic review of research on ethical dilemmas in crisis management. Development of the theoretical framework.

Phase 2
Current

Interviews & Game Development

Conducting in-depth interviews with practitioners in crisis management. Their real-world experiences directly inform the game's scenarios and decision architecture. Simultaneously designing and developing the simulation game.

Phase 3

Playtesting & Iteration

Testing with practitioners and stakeholders. Iterating on game mechanics, scenarios, and the ethical dilemma framework.

Phase 4

Research Studies

Conducting studies using the game to investigate how ethical dilemmas are handled in practice and what consequences different priorities have for crisis response.

Phase 5

Game Release & Open Data

Public release of the game, research data, and accompanying analysis as open resources for education, training, and further research.

Collaboration

We welcome collaboration with researchers and practitioners who share our interest in ethical decision-making, crisis management, serious games, and pedagogical innovation. Whether you are interested in contributing to the current project or exploring new directions together — such as adapting the game framework for different crisis contexts, conducting comparative studies across countries, or developing complementary research on moral stress and resilience — we are eager to connect and explore possibilities.

To discuss potential collaborations, please contact Joel Martinsson.

More information about publications, research data, game mechanics for research, and related topics will be published here as the project progresses.
