About the game

What is Saving of Swedovia?

Saving of Swedovia is an interactive research game that explores how people navigate ethical dilemmas during climate hazards. Players take on the roles of politicians, civil servants and first responders in the fictional country of Swedovia, facing difficult decisions where competing values clash and there are no clear right answers.

The game is open access: free for anyone to play, and free to use in teaching and training. It brings together expertise in political science, game design and emergency management, and every decision made by consenting adult players helps researchers understand how different groups approach ethical trade-offs under pressure.

The project is generously funded by the Swedish Research Council and The Crafoord Foundation.

Behind the game

How Saving of Swedovia was made

The game is built on research with the people who make these decisions in real life.

  1. 01

    Interviews

    In-depth interviews with practitioners in crisis management about the ethical dilemmas they face.

  2. 02

    Survey

    A survey with practitioners, so the dilemmas in the game reflect real-world experiences.

  3. 03

    Game development

    The scenarios and decisions were designed and built into an interactive game.

  4. 04

    Playtesting

    Testing with practitioners and stakeholders, refining the scenarios and game mechanics.

  5. 05
    Early 2027

    Public release

    The game is released to the public and begins collecting data from consenting adult players.

  6. 06
    Late 2028

    Open data & research

    A fully anonymised dataset is released to the research community, and the main studies are published.

Education

Use Saving of Swedovia in teaching

The game is a ready-made starting point for discussions about ethics, priorities and responsibility in crisis management. It works in university courses, in professional training and in exercises for organisations that handle crises, and it is free to use.

1

Play

Participants play individually, or together as a group on one screen.

2

Discuss

Talk through the choices: which values clashed, what was prioritised, and why.

3

Compare

Compare your choices with how other players and professions chose, using the data explorer.
