About the game

What is Saving of Swedovia?

Saving of Swedovia is an interactive research game that explores how people navigate ethical dilemmas during climate hazards. Players take on the roles of politicians, civil servants and first responders in the fictional country of Swedovia, facing difficult decisions where competing values clash and there are no clear right answers.

The game is open access: free for anyone to play, and free to use in teaching and training. It brings together expertise in political science, game design and emergency management, and every decision made by consenting adult players helps researchers understand how different groups approach ethical trade-offs under pressure.

The project is generously funded by the Swedish Research Council and The Crafoord Foundation.

How Saving of Swedovia was made

  1. 01

    Interviews

    We interviewed practitioners in crisis management about the ethical dilemmas they face.

  2. 02

    Survey

    A survey with practitioners made sure the dilemmas reflect real-world experiences.

  3. 03

    Game development

    The scenarios and decisions were designed and built into an interactive game.

  4. 04

    Playtesting

    The game was tested with practitioners and stakeholders, and refined along the way.

  5. 05

    Release

    In 2027 the game was released to the public, free for everyone to play.

Education

Use Saving of Swedovia in teaching

  • Free to use
  • No account needed
  • Swedish and English
  • Alone or in groups

The game is a ready-made starting point for discussions about ethics, priorities and responsibility in crisis management. It works in university courses, in professional training and in exercises for organisations that handle crises. It is completely free: anyone may use it in teaching and training, without a licence, an account or any cost.

A typical session has three parts. Participants first play, individually or together as a group on one screen. Then the group talks through the choices: which values clashed, what was prioritised, and why. Finally, participants can compare their choices with how other players and professions chose, using the data explorer.

To protect participants, results are only shown when at least 5 answers match. A fully anonymised dataset will be released to the research community, planned for late 2028.
