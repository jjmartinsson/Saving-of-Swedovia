About the game

What is Saving of Swedovia?

Saving of Swedovia is an interactive research game that explores how people think ethical dilemmas during natural hazards should be handled. A wildfire is spreading in the fictional town of Swedovia. You play a newly recruited ethical adviser at VERITAS, an independent group called in to guide the police, the rescue services, healthcare, and politicians and civil servants. They ask what they should do when values clash and there is no clear right answer. You advise, they act.

Real risk, real chance

Some options come with a stated risk. When Oskar’s officers can leave a drunk man in his flat during the evacuation, the option reads “30% risk that he dies in the fire”. If you choose it, the game draws the outcome at random with exactly that probability. Nothing is staged, so two players who make the same choice can get different results.

We built the game this way because risk and uncertainty are part of the dilemmas professionals face in a real crisis.

Natural hazards such as wildfires, floods and storms are becoming more frequent due to climate change. They can confront those who lead and carry out the response with hard ethical choices. The wildfire in Swedovia is one example, but the dilemmas are not unique to forest fires. Who should be helped first when resources run short? Should responders risk their lives to save others? When may a person's own choice be overridden? How honest should you be when the truth may cause harm? The same questions can arise in pandemics, major accidents, attacks and war.

The game is open access: free for anyone to play, and free to use in teaching and training. The choices made by adult players who agree to take part are used as research data, helping us understand how people think ethical dilemmas in a crisis should be handled.

The project is generously funded by the Swedish Research Council and The Crafoord Foundation.

A serious game, made for research

Saving of Swedovia is a serious game: a game made for a purpose beyond entertainment. We use the answers from the game in our research on how people think crises should be handled. That is why it matters that you choose the way you think the professionals in the game should handle the situations. There are no right or wrong answers.

Before the game starts, you get information about the research and how your answers are handled. You choose whether to take part.

From real experiences to a game

The scenarios in Saving of Swedovia were developed step by step, together with the professionals who make these decisions in real life.

1

Identifying dilemmas

We mapped the ethical dilemmas that can arise during natural hazards.

↻ Repeated until the scenarios were ready
2

Interviews

We interviewed professionals about the dilemmas and how they play out in practice.

3

Updating the scenarios

The scenarios were revised based on what the professionals told us.

4

Interviews again

Other professionals reviewed the updated scenarios, which built on the earlier feedback, and gave new feedback.

5

Finished scenarios

The scenarios were ready to be used in research.

Into the survey

The scenarios were turned into large-scale surveys, one for each profession: police, rescue services, healthcare, and politicians and civil servants. Each survey was sent to a representative sample of the Swedish population, about 4,000 people per survey. What Swedish citizens thought should be done was then built into the game itself.

Education

How to use Saving of Swedovia for teaching and practice

  • Free to use
  • No account needed
  • Swedish and English

The game is a ready-made starting point for discussions about ethics, priorities and responsibility in crisis management. It can be used by practitioners – police, rescue services, healthcare, politicians and civil servants – in training and exercises, and by students and citizens in schools, university courses and public discussions about how society should handle crises. It is completely free: anyone may use it, without a licence, an account or any cost.

A typical session has three parts. Participants first play, individually or together as a group on one screen. Then the group talks through the choices: which values clashed, what was prioritised, and why. Finally, participants can compare their choices with how other players and professions chose, using the data explorer.

For educators and practitioners

The educator guide

Our free educator guide helps you run a session with the game, whether you teach at a school or university or lead training for professionals. There is also in-depth material on parts of the game. The guide contains:

  • A two-hour session plan, with advice on playing alone or in groups
  • All six situations in each of the four missions, with questions for the discussion
  • Printable discussion sheets for small groups
  • An overview of the ten ethical principles in the game

Survey of Swedish citizens

Alongside the game, we carried out large-scale surveys built on the game's scenarios, one for each profession: police, rescue services, healthcare, and politicians and civil servants. Each survey was sent to a representative sample of the Swedish population, about 4,000 people per survey. What citizens thought should be done in each dilemma was then built into the game.
